1. Introduction

The lLocomotive rRouting pProblem (LRP) was firstly proposed by Huang et al., 2023, which and it is a variant of pPickup and dDelivery pProblem tTime wWindows with Last-in First-out LIFO [A2] constraints (PDPTWL). For transporting molten iron in the LRP, tTorpedo lLadle cCars (TPCs) are utilized. TPCs themselves are unable to move on their own and require locomotives for their transportation. TPCs play act as products and while locomotives play act as vehicles of in PDPTW[A3] L.

The m,Many-to-mMany lLocomotive rRouting pProblem (M2MLRP) covers not only one-to-one (1-1) requests but also one-to-many (1-M) and many-to-one (M-1) requests for the steel industryworks. M-1 requests are generated when there is a demand in some several placeslocations. In such cases, several TPCs that can fulfillfulfil the this demand are eligible for pick-up. On the contraryIn contrast, 1-M requests are generated when a newly available TPC is generated and needs to be delivered to a location within the a factory. The Additionally, 1-1 requests specify the exact TPC that needs to be picked up and its designated delivery location

The goal of M2MLRP is minimization to minimize of the total travel time of locomotives as when meeting all requests. Some Several constraints are required for the M2MLRP:.

Constraints:

1)      Compatibility (empty TPC and loaded TPCs can’t cannot [A4] be connected at the same time)

2)      LIFO

3)      Clearance

4)      Locomotive cCapacity (maximum of 2 TPCs)

5)      Loading time

6)      Time window of pickup and delivery

7)      Eligibility for the TPC destination of TPCs

 

2. Benchmark Problems

To the best our knowledge, the M2MLRP is firstly introduced in this study for the first time, and therefore, no available benchmark instances exists. A total of 40 instances were generated randomly to simulate a real-world factory in South Korea. These instances were categorised into three different sizes, representing varying levels of complexity (see Tables 1 and 2). In all the instances, the number of ironmaking facilities (IIFs), steelmaking facilities (SFs)[A5] , heaters (HR), buffer areas (BA), and repair factory (RF)iesy remained fixed with value of 5, 2, 1, 1, and 1, respectively. To model the factory layout, a road map was designed based on the actual environment.

To ensure safety and maintain continuous production, TPCs must be prepared under the IFs at least 5 minutes before the pouring process begins. Once the TPCs are fully loaded in the IFs, they need to be picked within 20 minutes and delivered to the SFs. In During preparation for the next workprocess, the empty TPCs generated after pouring molten iron in the SFs must also be picked up within 20 minutes. Pickup and delivery operations from RF and HR have a time window of 60 minutes. The lLoading and unloading service time  was set to 2 minutes, the locomotive capacity  was set to 2, and the loading time limit  was set to 120 minutes. The number of total number of TPCs in this study refers to the total number of TPCs considered in the problem formulation. It is important to note that this number does not necessarily reflect the actual number of TPCs in the steelworksteel-working processs. This study assumes that new, empty TPCs are generated from the SFs and are consumed in the IFs, while loaded TPCs are generated from the IFs and consumed in the SFs. Therefore, the number of TPCs can vary depending on the duration of the considered work hours being considered.

The number of places locations in the problem formulation [A7] is influenced by the number of TPCs. Since Because the M2MLRP is a variant of PDPTW, it is necessary to distinguish between different destinations for each TPC. In cases where multiple TPCs have the same destination, the destination is split into multiple placeslocations. For example, if TPC1 and TPC2 both need to go to the same SF, the SF is split into SF1 and SF2, where SF1 is only eligible for TPC1 and SF2 is only eligible for TPC2. This allows forfor the proper differentiation and assignment of TPCs to their respective destinations.

 

Instance Generation Process

To make generate instances, we initially examined the production schedule of ironmaking facilities (blast furnace). In a real-world scenarios, the bottleneck is often the tasks in ironmaking facilitiesIFs often represent the bottleneck in production processes. Therefore, the production schedule for steelmaking facilities,SFs which follows the ironmaking facilities, should consider the schedulethat of the ironmaking facilitiesIFs. Subsequently, requests for related tofrom the repair factorythe repair factoryRF  and heaterHRer, which are relatively independent, were determined sequentially[A8] . After establishing the production schedules, we generated information about concerning TPCs from considering steelmaking facilitiesSFs, buffer areasBA, heatersHR, repair factoriesRF,y and ironmaking facilitiesIFs. The instance generation process was defined as follows.

1.       Creating production schedules for iron-making facilities[A9] IFs: Initially, we generated random schedules for each ironmaking facilityIF, with values chosen from the set (0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40)[A10]  (min). Then, we created subsequent schedules for each ironmaking facilityIF by adding 40 minutes, which is the production duration of each ironmaking facility IF. For example, if the first production schedule for ironmaking facilityIF ‘A’ is set at 15, the second schedule for ‘A’ is set at 15 + 40 = 55, the third is 95, and so on. Those production schedules were then utilised used to generate demand requests in ironmaking facilitiesIFs. We set stipulated that a TPC should arrive between 25 minutes to 5 minutes before production. For instance, if the production time was set at 30, the time window for the demand is [5,25]. To prevent excessively tight time windows, production schedules with a production time of lower less than 25 were excluded.

2.       Creating production schedules for steel-making facilities[A11] SFs: Since Because five loaded TPCs are generated from the five iron-making facilitiesIFs working during a 40-minute interval, an empty TPC is generated with an 8-minute interval from steelmaking facilitiesSFs. The first production schedule was randomly chosen from the interval [0,8]. Subsequent production schedules were then created by adding 8 minutes. To avoid too excessively tight time windows, production schedules with a production time lower of less than 20 were excluded.

3.       Generating requests for the repair factoryRF and heaterHR: For To generateing requests for the repair factoryRF, we first determined whether repair requests are to be considered. Repair schedules are randomly calculated with the range of [0,24h] h. If the starting time for necessary repairs is less than the number of working hours minus 60 minutes, the repair schedule is included as a request in the instance. For creating requests for the heater, we initially determine the number of vacancies in the heaterHR, employing the same mechanism as used for the repair factoryRF. The heating schedule is then calculated within the range of [0, 6h] h for each vacancy.

4.       Generating TPCs from steelmaking facilitiesSFs: TPCs are supplied from steelmaking facilitiesSFs. We initiated the supply of TPCs based on the production schedule of steelmaking facilitiesSFs.

5.       Generating TPCs in the buffer areaBA: A total of 20 TPCs are determined used with their respectivevarying characteristics. TPCs in the buffer areaBA exhibit variations in temperature; certain TPCs may be sufficiently hot to transport molten iron to ironmaking facilitiesIFs, while others may be cold and require transportation to the heater for reheating. Additionally, some cold TPCs remain on standby in the buffer areaBA for potential repairs.

6.       Generating TPCs in the heater HR and repair factoryRF: TPCs become available from the heater HR or repair factoryRF once their assigned tasks are completed. The available times are randomly chosen within the range of 0 to half of the0.5 working hours, with a time window interval of 60 minutes.

7.       Generating TPCs from ironmaking facilitiesIFs: Considering the production schedules of ironmaking facilitiesIFs, we generated loaded TPCs. The time windows were set between 0 to 20 minutes after the production was finished.

8.       Creating a distance matrix: Distances between each facility were generated using the values shown in Table 3.

9.       Generating locomotives: The available locations of each locomotive were randomly set among all facilities, and their available time was set to 0.
